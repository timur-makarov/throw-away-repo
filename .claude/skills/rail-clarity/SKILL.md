# Get to the real goal

- **Assumes:** a person is present and can answer. They have a problem or a wish, not yet a request.
- **Produces:** a short statement in the chat: the real goal, the insight that made it clear, and the next
  steps, smallest first. No ticket and no code.
- **Done means:** the person confirms the statement. Stopping here is a complete outcome.

Most stuck requests solve the wrong problem. The question underneath every round is: what are you really
trying to do?

## How to ask

1. **Restate** the topic in one sentence. Then ask what they are really trying to do, and offer their
   stated goal next to two or three deeper goals it might stand for.
2. **Ask in rounds.** Ask only what the next step needs, one to three questions at a time. Give each
   question concrete options and mark the one you would pick, with one line of why, so "yes" is a full
   answer.
3. **Read the code and the data yourself.** Never ask what the repository can answer.
4. **Say what you hear** between rounds: a pattern, or two answers that contradict each other.
5. **Stop at the turn.** When the answers become decisive, or the person says something like "oh, actually",
   stop asking.
6. **If the talk repeats** without new information, take a position: "I would do X, because Y". Ask them
   to react to it.

Weigh the small words in what the person says. "Just", "only" and "quick" set a ceiling on the size of the
answer. "Still" means something was tried and failed, so the obvious cause is suspect. "Again" means it came
back, so the cause matters more than the symptom.

Question the premise at least once. "Do you need this at all?" is often the most useful question.

## Close

Write the statement. Then recommend one next step and name it: `rail-deepen` when the goal is clear and
the request is still shallow; `rail-grill` when it is ready to become a ticket; nothing when the answer was
a decision and no work follows. Go on only when the person says so, or asked for it at the start.
